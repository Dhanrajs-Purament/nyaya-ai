# Constitution of India — Fundamental Rights & Arrest Safeguards

Source: Legislative Department, Ministry of Law and Justice (legislative.gov.in).

## Fundamental Rights (Part III)
- **Article 14** — Equality before law and equal protection of the laws.
- **Article 15** — No discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth.
- **Article 19** — Freedoms: speech and expression; peaceful assembly; association; movement; residence; profession (subject to reasonable restrictions).
- **Article 20** — Protection in respect of conviction: no ex post facto punishment, **no double jeopardy**, **no self-incrimination**.
- **Article 21** — **Right to life and personal liberty**; includes right to privacy, dignity, speedy trial, legal aid, health (as interpreted by the Supreme Court).
- **Article 21A** — Free and compulsory education for children 6–14 years.
- **Article 22** — Protection against arrest and detention: right to be informed of grounds of arrest, to consult a lawyer of choice, and **production before a Magistrate within 24 hours**.
- **Articles 23–24** — Prohibition of human trafficking, forced labour, and child labour (under 14 in hazardous work).
- **Articles 25–28** — Freedom of religion.
- **Article 32** — Right to move the **Supreme Court** directly for enforcement of fundamental rights (writs: habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto, certiorari). **Article 226** — same power for High Courts (wider scope).
- **Article 39A** — State must provide **free legal aid** so that justice is not denied due to poverty.

## D.K. Basu guidelines (Supreme Court, arrest/detention)
Police must wear visible ID; prepare an **arrest memo** attested by a family member/witness and signed by the arrestee with date & time; inform a relative/friend; allow lawyer visits; medical examination every 48 hours in custody; entries in the case diary. Violations invite contempt of court and departmental action. These are now largely codified in BNSS Sections 35–58.

## Habeas corpus (illegal detention)
If a person is illegally detained (by police or anyone), family can file a **habeas corpus writ petition** in the High Court (Article 226) or Supreme Court (Article 32) for immediate production and release.
