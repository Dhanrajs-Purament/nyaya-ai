# Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA) — Law of Evidence

Act No. 47 of 2023. In force from 1 July 2024. **Replaces the Indian Evidence Act, 1872.** 170 sections. Source: India Code, Ministry of Home Affairs.

## Key principles
- Applies to all judicial proceedings including courts-martial, but not to affidavits or arbitration (Section 1).
- Evidence includes **electronic and digital records** (statements given electronically count as evidence — Section 2).

## Electronic evidence (major modernisation)
- **Electronic records are primary evidence** (Section 57); admissible like paper documents (Section 61).
- Electronic record admissibility requires a **certificate under Section 63(4)** (successor of old Section 65B certificate), signed by the person in charge of the device/system and an expert.
- Covers records on semiconductor memory, smartphones, emails, server logs, locational evidence, voice mails.

## Confessions & admissions (Sections 15–24)
- **Confession to a police officer is inadmissible** (Section 23(1)).
- Confession while in police custody is inadmissible unless made in the immediate presence of a Magistrate (Section 23(2)).
- **Discovery statements**: the part of information leading to discovery of a fact may be proved (Section 23(3), old 27 Evidence Act).
- Confession caused by inducement, threat, coercion or promise is irrelevant (Section 22).

## Dying declaration (Section 26)
Statement by a person about the cause of their death is relevant evidence even without oath/cross-examination.

## Burden of proof (Sections 104–119)
- Whoever asserts a fact must prove it (Section 104); burden of proving guilt is on the prosecution.
- Special knowledge: burden on the person who has it (Section 109).
- Presumptions: legitimacy of child (116), abetment of suicide of married woman within 7 years (117), **dowry death presumption** (118), absence of consent in certain rape prosecutions (Section 120).

## Witnesses & examination (Sections 124–168)
- Who may testify; privileged communications: spousal (128), judges (127), **professional communications with lawyers (132)**, state affairs (129).
- Examination-in-chief, cross-examination, re-examination (Sections 140–146); leading questions; hostile witnesses; refreshing memory.
- No new trial merely for improper admission/rejection of evidence (Section 169).
