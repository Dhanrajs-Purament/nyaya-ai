# Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS) — Police Procedure, FIR, Arrest, Bail

Act No. 46 of 2023. In force from 1 July 2024. **Replaces the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (CrPC).** 531 sections. Source: India Code, Ministry of Home Affairs.

## FIR — First Information Report (Section 173)
- FIR can be filed at **any police station regardless of where the offence happened (Zero FIR)**; it is then transferred to the correct station.
- FIR can be given **electronically (e-FIR)**; it must be signed within 3 days.
- A **free copy of the FIR** must be given to the informant/victim immediately (Section 173(2)).
- For offences punishable 3–7 years, police may conduct a **preliminary enquiry within 14 days** (with senior officer approval) to check if a prima facie case exists.
- If police refuse to register an FIR: send the complaint in writing to the **Superintendent of Police** (Section 173(4)); if still refused, apply to the **Magistrate under Section 175(3)**.
- Victims are entitled to a **progress report of the investigation within 90 days** (Section 193).

## Arrest — rights of the arrested person
- **Grounds of arrest must be communicated** (Section 47).
- **Right to have a relative/friend informed** of the arrest and place of detention (Section 48); arrest details must be displayed at the police station and district HQ (Section 37).
- **Production before a Magistrate within 24 hours** of arrest (Section 58).
- **Medical examination** after arrest (Sections 51–53).
- **No arrest of a woman after sunset and before sunrise** except exceptional circumstances with prior Magistrate permission (Section 43(5)); women to be searched only by women officers with decency (Section 43).
- For offences punishable under 3 years, arrest of an **infirm or 60+ year old person needs prior permission of an officer not below DSP** (Section 35(7)).
- **Notice of appearance instead of arrest** (Section 35(3)) for offences up to 7 years — police may require attendance without arresting; arrest then needs recorded reasons.
- Handcuffs only for habitual/repeat offenders or serious listed offences (Section 43(3)).
- Right to a lawyer of choice during interrogation (Section 38) and to **free legal aid**.

## Police custody & detention limits (Section 187)
- Police custody: up to **15 days total** (can be sought in parts during first 40/60 days).
- Judicial custody limits before chargesheet: **90 days** (offences punishable with death/life/10+ years), **60 days** (others) — after this, the accused has a **right to default bail**.

## Bail (Sections 478–483)
- Bailable offences: bail is a **right**.
- **First-time offender who has served one-third of the maximum sentence** as an undertrial must be released on bond (Section 479); undertrials who served half the maximum sentence (non-capital offences) must be released.
- Anticipatory bail continues (Section 482).

## Investigation & trial safeguards
- **Search and seizure must be videographed** (Section 105).
- **Forensic team visit is mandatory** for offences punishable with 7+ years (Section 176(3)).
- Chargesheet within 60/90 days; further investigation beyond 90 days needs court permission.
- **Judgment within 45 days** after completion of arguments (Section 258); charges framed within 60 days of first hearing.
- Trials and proceedings can be held in **electronic mode** (Section 532); summons can be served electronically (Section 63).
- **In-absentia trial** of proclaimed absconders (Section 356).
- **Witness Protection Scheme** mandated (Section 398).
- Mercy petitions timelines defined (Section 472).
