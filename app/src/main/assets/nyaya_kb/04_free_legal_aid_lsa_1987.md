# Free Legal Aid — Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 (NALSA)

Act No. 39 of 1987. Gives effect to **Article 39A of the Constitution** (equal justice and free legal aid). Source: India Code / nalsa.gov.in.

## Who is entitled to FREE legal services (Section 12)
- **Women and children (all, regardless of income)**
- Members of **Scheduled Castes / Scheduled Tribes**
- Victims of **human trafficking or begar** (forced labour)
- Persons with **disability**
- Victims of **mass disaster, ethnic violence, caste atrocity, flood, drought, earthquake, industrial disaster**
- **Industrial workmen**
- Persons **in custody** (including protective homes, juvenile homes, psychiatric hospitals)
- Any person whose **annual income is below the prescribed limit** (varies by state; e.g., ₹5 lakh for Supreme Court Legal Services Committee)

## What free legal services include
- A **free lawyer** to represent you in any court case (defence or filing)
- Payment of **court fees, process fees**, and all charges
- **Drafting of legal documents**, appeals, paper books
- Legal advice at front offices of legal services authorities

## Structure
- **NALSA** (national) → **State Legal Services Authorities (SLSA)** → **District Legal Services Authorities (DLSA)** in every district → **Taluk Legal Services Committees**; Supreme Court and High Court Legal Services Committees for those courts.

## How to apply
1. Call **15100** (NALSA helpline), or
2. Visit the **DLSA front office** in your District Court complex, or
3. Apply online at **nalsa.gov.in** (Legal Aid Application), or write a simple application on paper — no fee, no format required.

## Lok Adalats (Sections 19–22)
- Settle disputes by compromise (civil cases, compoundable criminal cases, bank recovery, motor accident claims, family disputes, utility bills).
- **Award of a Lok Adalat is final, binding, and deemed a decree of a civil court**; no appeal; court fee is refunded if a pending case is settled.
- **Permanent Lok Adalats (Section 22B)** for public utility services (transport, post, water, electricity, hospitals) — can decide on merits up to ₹1 crore if no settlement.
