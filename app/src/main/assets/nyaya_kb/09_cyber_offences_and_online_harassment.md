# Online Harassment, Leaked Photos, Cyber Crime & Digital Abuse — What the Law Says and What To Do

Everyday words people use: my photos were leaked, someone leaked my private pictures, revenge porn, morphed photos, deepfake, nude photos shared without permission, MMS leaked, screenshot of my chats shared, someone is blackmailing me with my photos, sextortion, online harassment, trolling, abusive messages, threats on WhatsApp, someone made a fake profile of me, catfishing, my Instagram was hacked, someone is stalking me online, cyberbullying, obscene messages, unwanted video call, doxxing, my number was posted online.

This file maps that everyday language onto the actual Indian sections, because the bare acts use very different words ("captures, publishes or transmits the image of a private area", "electronic form").

## Do this first (practical steps)

1. **Report online at the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal: cybercrime.gov.in.** It has a dedicated, confidential category for "Women/Child Related Crime" including non-consensual intimate images. You can report anonymously.
2. **Cyber crime helpline: 1930** (national). **Emergency / immediate danger: 112.** **Women helpline: 181.** **Children: CHILDLINE 1098.**
3. **Preserve evidence before anything else.** Take screenshots showing the URL/profile name/date, save the chat, note phone numbers and usernames. Do not delete the messages, even if they are painful to keep — they are your proof.
4. **Demand takedown.** Under the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021, a platform must remove content that shows a person in a sexual act, or in nudity or partial nudity, or in an impersonated/morphed form, **within 24 hours** of a complaint by the person affected or someone on their behalf. Use the platform's grievance officer and keep the complaint reference.
5. **You can file an FIR at any police station (Zero FIR)** — see BNSS Section 173. If the police refuse, complain in writing to the Superintendent of Police under Section 173(4), and then to the Magistrate under Section 175(3).
6. **Free lawyer:** NALSA / District Legal Services Authority, helpline **15100**. You do not have to pay a lawyer to pursue this.

## Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS) — the criminal offences

- **Section 77 — Voyeurism.** Watching or capturing the image of a woman engaged in a private act where she would not expect to be observed, or sharing such an image. This is the core section for secretly recorded or leaked private images.
- **Section 78 — Stalking.** Repeatedly following, contacting or attempting to contact a woman despite her disinterest, **including monitoring her use of the internet, email or any other electronic communication**. This covers persistent DMs, fake accounts, and tracking someone online.
- **Section 75 — Sexual harassment.** Includes unwelcome sexual advances and sexually coloured remarks; showing pornography against a woman's will.
- **Section 351 — Criminal intimidation.** Threatening someone with injury to person, reputation or property. This is the section for "I will leak your photos unless you..." — that is blackmail by threat, i.e. sextortion.
- **Section 356 — Defamation.** Publishing an imputation intending to harm someone's reputation.
- Extortion and criminal breach of trust provisions may also apply where money is demanded.

## Information Technology Act, 2000 — the cyber offences

- **Section 66E — Punishment for violation of privacy.** Intentionally capturing, publishing or transmitting the image of a **private area of any person** without consent, in circumstances violating their privacy. Note this section protects **any person**, not only women.
- **Section 67 — Publishing or transmitting obscene material in electronic form.**
- **Section 67A — Publishing or transmitting material containing a sexually explicit act.**
- **Section 67B — Publishing or transmitting material depicting children in a sexually explicit act.** See the child-safety note below.
- **Section 66C — Identity theft** (fraudulent use of someone's electronic signature, password or unique identification feature).
- **Section 66D — Cheating by personation using a computer resource.** This is the section for fake profiles and impersonation scams.
- **Section 66 — Computer related offences** (read with Section 43): unauthorised access, hacking an account, downloading data, damaging data.
- **Section 66F — Cyber terrorism.**
- **Section 67C — Preservation and retention of information by intermediaries**, which is why acting quickly helps: platform logs are not kept forever.
- **Section 72 — Breach of confidentiality and privacy** by a person acting under powers of the Act.

**Important — Section 66A is NOT valid law.** Section 66A (offensive messages sent through a communication service) was struck down as unconstitutional by the Supreme Court of India in *Shreya Singhal v. Union of India* (2015). It still appears in printed copies of the bare Act, and cases are still sometimes wrongly registered under it. No one can be charged under Section 66A. If a police officer cites it, say so and ask for the correct section.

## Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

If an organisation (not an individual) leaked your personal data, the DPDP Act, 2023 gives you rights against that Data Fiduciary, including the right to erasure and the right to grievance redressal, with complaints to the Data Protection Board. This is a separate track from the criminal complaint and you can pursue both.

## If the person in the images is under 18

Treat this as urgent and separate. Sexual content involving anyone under 18 is child sexual abuse material.

- Report immediately to **cybercrime.gov.in**, the police, or **CHILDLINE 1098**.
- The Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, 2012 applies alongside **IT Act Section 67B**, and reporting is mandatory for anyone who knows about it.
- **Do not forward, download, or share the material to "prove" anything, even to a friend or to a group** — passing it on is itself a serious offence. Describe it to the police instead and let them collect it.
- A child should be brought to a trusted adult and to the police or CHILDLINE, never handled privately between the child and the person who has the images.

## What to tell someone who is scared

Being targeted this way is not the victim's fault, and it is not something they have to accept. The law is on their side, reporting is free, and the identity of a complainant in these categories is protected. Encourage them to involve one trusted adult or friend rather than dealing with it alone, and to get the free NALSA lawyer on 15100 rather than paying anyone who promises to "delete everything" for a fee — that offer is itself usually a scam.
